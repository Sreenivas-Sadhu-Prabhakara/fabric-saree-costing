How it works

Yarn is bought by the kilogram, so the warp and weft weights are added and divided by 1,000 to turn grams into kilograms, then multiplied by the yarn rate per kg to give the yarn cost. Wastage grosses up the yarn cost only — it covers thread lost to sizing, broken ends and cutting — and leaves the job-work and labour figures untouched. Dyeing or printing, embroidery or other job-work, labour and overhead are each added as a flat amount per unit. Adding them to the grossed-up yarn gives the true cost, which is your break-even. The selling price is the true cost plus your margin percentage.

In short: yarn = (warp + weft) ÷ 1000 × rate/kg, grossed up by wastage, then true cost = yarn-after-wastage + dyeing + embroidery + labour + overhead and price = true cost × (1 + margin%).

Assumptions & rates
  • Yarn is priced per kilogram; warp and weft are entered in grams and converted at 1,000 g = 1 kg.
  • Wastage applies to the yarn cost only, never to job-work, labour or overhead.
  • Dyeing / printing, embroidery / job-work, labour and overhead are flat amounts per unit.
  • Break-even equals the true cost; margin is taken on the true cost.
  • The margin-bands table shows the selling price at 10%, 20%, 30% and 40% on the true cost.
